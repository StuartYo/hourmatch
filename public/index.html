HourMatch

Renewable Energy & Energy Trading track

A factory can buy enough solar certificates to say "100% renewable" while running on gas every night. HourMatch issues renewable certificates per plant per hour on a shared ledger, lets each one be retired once and only against the same hour's metered use, then checks any green-power claim against that ledger.

Demo data. Every company, power plant and contract here is fictional. The hourly solar, wind and hydro output curves are real: Taiwan Power Company open data, 6 to 12 October 2026 (data.gov.tw dataset 8931), scaled down to plant size.

Why a blockchain and not a database

One retirement state, many issuersA certificate body, a plant selling directly to a factory and a utility reselling green power each keep their own books today. The same megawatt-hour can be counted in two of them. One shared contract holds the only "retired" flag.
Anyone can re-check a claimmatchedBps(consumer, from, to) is a public read. An NGO, an auditor or a customer recomputes the share without an account or a data request.
Claims cannot be edited lateranchorClaim stores the claim's hash together with a share computed by the contract itself, not supplied by the company.

Limit, stated plainly: if a single national registry published hourly certificates and opened read access, it could do most of this without a chain. Taiwan's registry issues certificates per 1,000 kWh today, not per hour.
